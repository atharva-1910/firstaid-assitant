## Findings

**1. Rules + model: the model catches what the word lists miss (E1, all).** Before the probe-driven lexicon fixes, the rules missed 8 probe emergencies. A TF-IDF model trained only on template data, added under the rules floor (it may only raise urgency), brings that to 0, with no lexicon changes. The cost is false alarms: 50% of the 20 probe non-emergencies are flagged (rules alone: 0%). On the safety suite the models alone miss 7 emergencies, so the rules floor stays essential. Figure: `figures/e1_probe_emergency_recall.png`.

**2. Hindi and Marathi need their own data; Hinglish borrows from English (E2, Taneesha).** With English training data only, classical features reach 0.45 situation accuracy on Hinglish probe messages and 0.00 on Hindi and Marathi (no shared script). Adding 5% of the target language (36 messages) gives 0.88 / 0.86 / 0.82 (Hindi / Hinglish / Marathi); all of it reaches only 0.91 / 0.95 / 0.86 on safety-suite and probe messages against 1.00 on template dev. Marathi has the largest template-to-real gap. Notebook 05 repeats the curve with multilingual embeddings, where 0% is true zero-shot. Figure: `figures/e2_transfer.png`.

**3. Under realistic noise the rules collapse, models degrade gracefully (E3, all).** With every noise type at level 0.3 (vowels dropped, typos, spelling variants, code-switching, dropped words), emergency recall on safety-suite and probe messages falls to 0.24 for the rules but stays at 0.96 for the character model and 0.97 for the hybrid; situation accuracy falls to 0.50 (rules) and 0.76 (character model). The models' false-alarm rate is high throughout (see `e3_robustness.md`), so the hybrid trades precision for safety. Dropped vowels and dropped words hurt the rules most. Figure: `figures/e3_robustness.png`.

**4. The template corpus is narrow (E4).** 720 English template messages use 135 distinct words; 30 team-written safety-suite messages use 129. Size-matched (24 messages), templates have ~87 distinct words against ~109 team-written. Hinglish Code-Mixing Index: templates 5.9 (43% of messages mixed), probe 6.3 (28%). Figure: `figures/e4_corpus.png`.

**5. The 0.75 confidence threshold is well placed (E5, Atharva).** On safety-suite and probe messages the model's ECE is 0.051. Messages above 0.75 are 0.96 correct, those below only 0.63: asking a question there is the right call. The app asked a question for 27% of hand-written messages. Figure: `figures/e5_calibration.png`.

**6. Negation direction matters; uncertainty needs a wider scope (E6, Siddhi).** On the diagnostic set, applying the English rule (cue before the word) to Hindi, Hinglish and Marathi drops fact accuracy to 0.62 / 0.62 / 0.64 with 78 dangerous errors; the current direction-aware rule gets 0.81 / 0.81 / 0.89 with 9. The remaining dangerous errors come from uncertainty cues attaching to the nearest word ("not sure if **my father** is breathing" read as breathing normally). Letting uncertainty cover every safety fact in its clause raises accuracy to 0.95 with 0 dangerous errors and no change on the safety suite or probe; a full ConText-style scope gets 0.86. The last misses ("कोई दर्द या दबाव नहीं") are lexicon gaps, not negation. The diagnostic set was drafted by Claude; the owners' 100-sentence sets are needed to confirm. Figure: `figures/e6_negation.png`.

**7. Word overlap cannot verify a rephrased step (E7, Siddhi).** Across 1309 step/candidate pairs in four languages, a word-overlap verifier tuned to accept 95% of safe rewordings rejects only 11% of steps with "do not" removed and 6% with a changed number ("20 minutes" → "2 minutes"); character F-score rejects 45% and 12%. A guard written for exactly these edits catches them, but still misses 39% of antonym swaps and rejects 32% of harmless polite forms. This is the case for the trained mmBERT verifier; notebook 05 scores it on the same pairs. Figure: `figures/e7_verifier.png`.

**8. Highlighted words are faithful, but the app's F4 words are incomplete (E8, Soham).** Deleting a model's top-3 attributed words lowers P(emergency) by 0.26 on average, against 0.03 for three random words. Deleting the app's own trigger words removes the rule alert in 84% of emergencies: in the rest, other words also fire the rules but are not shown. The character model's top word is one of the app's trigger words in 78% of messages. Notebook 05 adds Integrated Gradients for IndicBERT v2 and a rating sheet for plausibility. Figure: `figures/e8_faithfulness.png`.
